Section OneIntroduction

1.1  About This Manual

This Owner's Manual describes the safe installation, operation, and maintenance of the DELTA-ECO delta robot. It is intended for engineers, integrators, operators, and maintenance technicians. Keep it with the machine for the life of the equipment.

1.2  Product Overview

DELTA-ECO is Lebanon's first open-architecture delta robotic arm — a high-speed, three-arm parallel robot built for real industrial material handling. It was developed by Team JC2M at the American University of Science & Technology in partnership with Raymond Feghali Co.

Unlike conventional industrial robots, DELTA-ECO replaces the traditional PLC and dedicated hardware motion controller with a single industrial PC running a real-time Linux kernel. Deterministic motion is delivered over an EtherCAT fieldbus, and AI-based machine vision allows the robot to locate and pick objects dynamically rather than from fixed taught points.

1.3  Intended Use

DELTA-ECO is designed for automated handling of lightweight objects within its defined work envelope, in a controlled industrial or laboratory environment, operated by trained personnel. Supported tasks include:

  • Pick-and-place — high-frequency transfer of parts with a vacuum or mechanical gripper.
  • Palletizing & depalletizing — structured row × column × level stacking and destacking.
  • Transfer & sequencing — coordinated multi-station handling routines.
  • Vision-guided picking — locating objects on a moving conveyor in real time.

1.4  Limitations of Use

Warning — Do Not Use For

Payloads above 3 kg; operation outside the defined work envelope or environmental limits; explosive or flammable atmospheres; lifting or transport of persons; or unguarded human-collaborative operation. DELTA-ECO is a developmental unit and must be operated only under qualified supervision.

1.5  Manual Conventions

Safety messages in this manual follow a four-level system — DANGER, WARNING, CAUTION, and NOTICE. These are defined in Section 2.1. Always observe the action described in each safety message.

Section TwoSafety
Danger — High-Speed Moving Machinery

DELTA-ECO moves its arm and end-effector at speeds up to 9 m/s. Contact with a moving part can cause serious crushing or impact injury. Never enter the work envelope while the robot is powered and not in a confirmed safe state.

2.1  Safety Alert Symbols

This manual uses the following standard safety message levels:

⚠
DANGER
Imminent hazard which, if not avoided, will result in death or serious injury.
⚠
WARNING
Potential hazard which, if not avoided, could result in death or serious injury.
⚠
CAUTION
Potential hazard which, if not avoided, may result in minor or moderate injury.
ⓘ
NOTICE
Practice not related to personal injury; risk of equipment damage or data loss.

2.2  General Safety Rules

  • Only personnel trained on this manual may install, operate, or service the robot.
  • The work envelope must be guarded — fencing, an interlocked enclosure, or a light curtain — before any automatic operation.
  • Keep all body parts, tools, and loose material clear of the envelope during operation.
  • Know the location of the Emergency Stop before starting the machine (see 2.4).
  • Never bypass, defeat, or remove guards, interlocks, or safety devices.
  • Do not operate a robot that is damaged, making unusual noise, or behaving unexpectedly.
  • Maintain a clean, dry, well-lit, and uncluttered area around the machine.

2.3  Residual Hazards

Even when operated correctly, the following hazards remain present:

  • Crush / impact — the bicep and forearm joints sweep a large volume at high speed.
  • Pinch points — at the elbow joints and at the moving platform / end-effector.
  • Electrical hazard — the servo drives and control PC operate at mains voltage.
  • Stored energy — the arm may settle under gravity when drive power is removed.
  • Unexpected restart — the robot may move when a fault is cleared or a cycle resumes.

2.4  Emergency Stop

The Emergency Stop (E-STOP) immediately removes motion power and brings all three drives to a stop. It is also available as a software control in the operator interface.

Warning

Test the E-STOP before every operating session. After an E-STOP, identify and correct the cause, confirm the envelope is clear, then re-enable the drives and re-home the robot before resuming. Do not use the E-STOP as a routine cycle-stop.

2.5  Personal Protective Equipment

  • Safety glasses when within the cell area.
  • Closed-toe protective footwear.
  • No loose clothing, neckties, lanyards, jewellery, or gloves near moving parts.
  • Long hair tied back and contained.

2.6  Lockout / Tagout (LOTO)

Before any mechanical inspection, cleaning, tool change, or repair inside the work envelope:

Stop the running cycle from the operator interface and place the robot in Idle.
Engage the Emergency Stop.
Switch off and lock out the main electrical disconnect; apply a tag identifying the person working.
Verify zero energy — confirm drives are unpowered and support the arm against gravity settle.
Only then enter the work envelope. Reverse the sequence to return to service.

4 · System Architecture DELTA-ECO
4
Section FourSystem Architecture

4.1  Mechanical Structure

DELTA-ECO uses a three-arm parallel (delta) kinematic structure. Three servo motors are fixed to the stationary base plate, 120° apart. Each motor drives a rigid bicep, which connects through a parallelogram pair of forearm rods to a small moving platform that carries the end-effector.

Because all three motors are mounted on the fixed base, the moving mass is very low — this is what allows a delta robot to reach high speed and acceleration while keeping the platform horizontal throughout its travel.

4.2  Control Architecture

The defining feature of DELTA-ECO is its open control architecture. A traditional delta cell uses a proprietary PLC plus a dedicated hardware motion controller. DELTA-ECO replaces that entire rack with a single industrial PC.

LayerTraditional cellDELTA-ECO
ControllerProprietary PLC / ASICIndustrial Linux PC
ProgrammingLadder / Structured TextNative C / CSP commands
Cycle time10–20 ms1 ms, jitter-free
Logic accessVendor lockedFully open

A real-time Linux kernel (PREEMPT_RT) with isolated CPU cores hosts the IgH EtherLab EtherCAT master. The DELTA-ECO motion daemon runs a deterministic 1 kHz loop: it reads encoder feedback, solves the inverse kinematics, and streams cyclic position commands to all three servo drives over EtherCAT — with sub-millisecond, distributed-clock-synchronised behaviour.

Result

What used to require a rack of dedicated hardware now runs on a single machine — with no vendor lock-in and no licence dependency.

4.3  Inverse Kinematics

Every Cartesian target (X, Y, Z) is converted by the custom C solver into the three joint angles θ1, θ2, θ3 required to place the platform at that point. The solver runs inside the 1 kHz loop, so the robot can be commanded directly in workspace coordinates — including coordinates supplied live by the vision system.

4.4  Software Stack

The control software is organised as five layers, from the operator-facing interface down to the servo hardware:

UI Operator GUI Custom control interface — modes, telemetry, manual control
1 kHz Motion Daemon Custom C — inverse kinematics & trajectory generation
BUS IgH EtherCAT Master Real-time fieldbus master, distributed clocks
OS PREEMPT_RT Linux Deterministic kernel with isolated CPU cores
HW Servo Drives ×3 Cyclic Synchronous Position (CSP) mode

4.5  Automation Pipeline

From sensor input to executed motion, five deterministic stages run on every control cycle:

01
Vision Capture
Camera captures the live conveyor scene as calibrated image data.
02
AI Detection
YOLO localises and classifies each target part in the scene.
03
IK Solver
C solver computes θ1, θ2, θ3 for every XYZ target.
04
EtherCAT Cycle
1 kHz loop streams CSP commands to all three drives.
05
Execution
The arm completes the pick, transfer or palletise motion.

4.6  Safety & Protective Functions

  • Soft joint limits — commands beyond the joint ranges in 3.3 are rejected.
  • Torque limits — drive torque is capped to protect the mechanism and payload.
  • Following-error monitoring — excessive position error triggers a controlled stop.
  • Jitter monitoring — loss of real-time determinism is detected and reported.
  • Emergency-stop integration — hardware and software E-STOP remove motion power.

5 · Installation DELTA-ECO
5
Section FiveInstallation & Commissioning
Warning

Installation and electrical connection must be carried out by qualified personnel only. Keep the robot de-energised until commissioning is complete.

5.1  Unpacking & Inspection

  • Inspect for transit damage before installation. Report any damage before proceeding.
  • Confirm all three arms, the moving platform, the end-effector, the control PC, and cabling are present.
  • Handle the forearm rods and ball joints with care — they are precision components.

5.2  Site Requirements

  • A rigid, level overhead frame able to carry the robot mass and dynamic reaction loads.
  • Clearance for the full work envelope (see Section 10) plus a guarded safety margin.
  • Indoor environment within the limits of Section 3.6.
  • A clean, stable power supply and a lockable main disconnect.

5.3  Installation Procedure

Mount the fixed base plate to the overhead frame, level, with the end-effector facing down. Torque all fasteners to specification.
Confirm the three biceps and forearm rods move freely through their full range without binding.
Fit and secure the end-effector to the moving platform; route its supply line with proper strain relief.
Connect the three servo motors to their drives; connect the drives in the EtherCAT chain to the control PC.
Connect the vision camera to the control PC and mount it over the work area (see Section 7).
Wire the Emergency Stop and any guarding interlocks. Verify mains earth/ground continuity.
Install guarding around the full work envelope before any powered motion.

5.4  First Power-Up & Commissioning

With the envelope clear, apply power and boot the control PC.
Launch the operator software and start the backend. Confirm EtherCAT: Online and all drives are detected.
Run Calibrate to home the robot. Verify the reported joint angles are sensible.
Jog each axis slowly in Manual mode and confirm correct, smooth motion within the envelope.
Test the Emergency Stop under motion. Record the commissioning data in Section 3.7.

Section SixOperation

6.1  The Operator Interface

DELTA-ECO is operated entirely from the custom control software, which presents the robot as a real industrial control station. The interface provides nine screens:

ScreenFunction
DashboardSystem status, cycle count, tool position, loop jitter.
Manual ControlDirect Cartesian jogging of the end-effector (X / Y / Z).
Pick and PlaceSingle pick → place transfer routines.
PalletizingFill a row × column × level grid from one pick point.
DepalletizeVision-guided destacking of mixed units.
TransferStation-to-station coordinated handling.
SequenceMulti-step routines chained into one program.
VisionLive camera feed and YOLO detection status.
SettingsCalibration, limits, and system configuration.

A persistent status bar shows Backend, EtherCAT, and Robot state, with Calibrate and Stop Backend controls. A right-side control panel keeps the Emergency Stop and live drive states visible at all times. For each of the three drives the software reports state, status word, mode, actual and command position, position error, velocity, and acceleration.

6.2  Start-Up Procedure

Complete the Pre-Operation Checklist (Section 2.7). Confirm the envelope is clear.
Power on the control enclosure and boot the PC; launch the operator software.
Start the backend — wait for Backend: Running and EtherCAT: Online.
Press Calibrate to home the robot. The drives transition from Not Ready to Ready.
Select the required operating mode. The robot is now ready for production.
Caution

The robot will move during calibration. Ensure the envelope is clear and guarded before pressing Calibrate.

6 · Operation DELTA-ECO

6.3  Operating Modes

Manual Control

Jog the end-effector directly along X, Y, and Z. The live tool position is displayed continuously. Use Manual mode for setup, teaching reference points, and verifying motion. Always jog at reduced speed near limits.

Pick-and-Place

The robot transfers an object from a pick point to a place point using the gripper. Pick and place coordinates can be entered numerically or captured from the current position.

Palletizing & Depalletizing

Define a Rows × Columns × Levels grid, the item dimensions, and X/Y spacing. From a single taught pick point the robot fills (or, in Depalletize, empties) the grid automatically with consistent spacing. Hand-captured manual place points are also supported.

Transfer & Sequence

Transfer performs coordinated station-to-station handling. Sequence chains multiple steps — picks, places, transfers — into a single repeatable program for multi-step routines.

6.4  During Operation

  • Monitor the Dashboard — cycle count, tool position, and loop jitter should remain stable.
  • Keep all personnel outside the guarded envelope while the robot is in automatic motion.
  • Stop immediately if you observe unusual noise, vibration, rising position error, or a jitter warning.
  • Do not exceed the 3 kg payload or command targets outside the work envelope.

6.5  Shutdown Procedure

Allow the current cycle to finish; place the robot in Idle.
Stop the backend from the operator software.
Shut down the control PC.
Switch off the control enclosure at the main disconnect.
Notice

For an immediate stop at any time, use the Emergency Stop. After an E-STOP, follow the recovery steps in Section 2.4 before resuming.

Section SevenVision System

DELTA-ECO sees before it moves. The vision system lets the robot adapt to object position dynamically, instead of relying on fixed pick points or mechanical guides.

7.1  Components

ItemDetail
CameraLogitech C170
Detection modelYOLO object detection
Processing frame size1280 × 720
OutputObject class + XYZ pick coordinate

7.2  How It Works

The camera captures the live conveyor scene as calibrated image data.
The YOLO model localises and classifies each target part directly from the running scene.
Each detection is converted into an XYZ coordinate in the robot's workspace.
The coordinate is fed straight into the inverse-kinematics solver for precision targeting.

7.3  Camera Mounting & Calibration

  • Mount the camera rigidly above the work area with an unobstructed view of the conveyor.
  • Provide stable, even lighting — avoid glare, shadows, and changing ambient light.
  • Calibrate the camera-to-robot coordinate mapping so detected pixels translate to accurate XYZ.
  • Re-verify calibration after any change to camera position, lens focus, or lighting.

7.4  Operating Notes

Notice

Detection accuracy depends on lighting and a clean lens. If detection confidence drops or parts are missed, check the camera connection, clean the lens, and confirm lighting before adjusting the model. Verify the vision-derived pick points stay inside the work envelope (Section 3.3).

Benefit

Because objects become robot targets directly, the line does not need fixed fixturing — the robot adapts to where the part actually is.

Section EightMaintenance
Warning

Apply the Lockout / Tagout procedure (Section 2.6) before any maintenance inside the work envelope. Never service the robot while it is powered.

8.1  Maintenance Schedule

IntervalTask
Each shift / dailyVisual inspection; confirm work area clear; test Emergency Stop; listen for unusual noise or vibration; check cable dressing.
WeeklyInspect ball joints and rod ends for play; check end-effector mounting; clean the camera lens; verify guarding and interlocks.
MonthlyCheck bicep and base fastener torque; inspect forearm rods and retention springs; inspect EtherCAT and motor cabling for wear.
Semi-annualFull fastener torque check; bearing and joint inspection; recalibrate the robot; recalibrate the vision system.
AnnualReview drive logs and jitter history; update / back up software configuration; replace worn ball joints or rods as required.

8.2  Inspection Points

  • Ball joints & forearm rods — the highest-wear parts. Replace any joint with detectable play or any bent rod.
  • Fasteners — high acceleration loosens hardware over time. Re-torque to specification.
  • Cabling — check the EtherCAT chain and motor leads for chafing, especially at flex points.
  • End-effector — confirm secure mounting and a functioning gripper / vacuum supply.
  • Camera — keep the lens clean; confirm the mount has not shifted.

8.3  After Maintenance

Following any mechanical work, re-home the robot with Calibrate, jog each axis slowly in Manual mode, verify the Emergency Stop, and run a low-speed test cycle before returning the machine to production.

Notice

Record completed maintenance, replaced parts, and recalibration dates in a machine log. A consistent log is the fastest route to diagnosing future faults.

9 · Troubleshooting DELTA-ECO
9
Section NineTroubleshooting

Use the table below for first-line diagnosis. If a fault persists after the corrective action, stop the machine, apply Lockout / Tagout, and contact the project team.

SymptomPossible causeCorrective action
EtherCAT shows Offline Cable unplugged, drive unpowered, or master not started. Check the fieldbus cabling and drive power; restart the backend.
Drive shows Not Ready Drives not enabled, or a fault is latched. Clear faults, run Calibrate, confirm drives transition to Ready.
Robot will not move in Manual Not homed, target outside soft limits, or E-STOP engaged. Release the E-STOP, calibrate, command a target inside the envelope.
Rising position / following error Excess payload, speed too high, or a mechanical bind. Reduce payload or speed; inspect joints and rods for binding.
Vision not detecting parts Camera disconnected, poor lighting, or model not loaded. Check the USB connection, improve lighting, clean the lens, reload the model.
High jitter warning Background CPU load, or core isolation lost. Close non-real-time processes; verify kernel core isolation.
Unexpected stop mid-cycle E-STOP, safety interlock, or a protective fault. Inspect guarding interlocks; review the system log; clear and resume per 2.4.
Unusual noise or vibration Loose fastener, worn ball joint, or end-effector imbalance. Stop, apply LOTO, inspect fasteners and joints; replace worn parts.
Inaccurate pick position Calibration drift, or vision mapping out of date. Re-home the robot; recalibrate the camera-to-robot mapping.
Warning

Never attempt to diagnose a fault by reaching into the work envelope while the robot is powered. De-energise and apply Lockout / Tagout first.

9.1  Diagnostic Resources

The operator software exposes a live system log, per-drive status words, real-time jitter and CPU-load telemetry, and the EtherCAT synchronisation state. Capture this information before contacting support — it greatly speeds up diagnosis.

Appendix A · Glossary DELTA-ECO
Appendix AGlossary of Terms
Delta robot
A parallel-kinematic robot in which three arms, all driven from a fixed base, support a single moving platform — giving very high speed and low moving mass.
Bicep
The rigid upper-arm link driven directly by a base-mounted servo motor.
Forearm
The pair of parallel rods linking each bicep to the moving platform; keeps the platform level throughout travel.
End-effector
The tool mounted on the moving platform — a vacuum or mechanical gripper — that handles the payload.
Work envelope
The full volume the end-effector can reach. See Section 3.3 and Fig. 3.
Repeatability
How closely the robot returns to the same point on repeated cycles. DELTA-ECO: ±0.8 mm.
IK — Inverse Kinematics
The computation that converts a Cartesian target (X, Y, Z) into the joint angles needed to reach it.
EtherCAT
A real-time industrial fieldbus used to connect the controller to the servo drives with deterministic timing.
Distributed Clocks
An EtherCAT mechanism that synchronises all devices on the bus to a common time base.
CSP — Cyclic Synchronous Position
A servo-drive control mode in which the controller sends a fresh target position every cycle.
PREEMPT_RT
A real-time patch for the Linux kernel that makes task timing deterministic enough for motion control.
IgH EtherLab
The open-source software that turns a Linux PC into an EtherCAT fieldbus master.
YOLO
A real-time AI object-detection method that locates and classifies objects in a camera image.
Jitter
Cycle-to-cycle variation in the timing of the control loop. Lower jitter means more deterministic motion.
HMI — Human–Machine Interface
The operator software through which the robot is configured, monitored, and controlled.
LOTO — Lockout / Tagout
The procedure for isolating and securing machine energy before maintenance. See Section 2.6.

Emergency Procedure

In an Emergency

Press the EMERGENCY STOP. Do not enter the work envelope until the robot is confirmed stopped and de-energised. Identify and correct the cause, confirm the envelope is clear, then re-enable the drives and re-home before resuming — see Section 2.4.

Daily Start-Up — At a Glance

  1. Complete the Pre-Operation Checklist (2.7) — envelope clear, E-STOP tested.
  2. Power on, boot the PC, launch the operator software.
  3. Start the backend — wait for EtherCAT: Online.
  4. Press Calibrate to home the robot.
  5. Select the operating mode and begin production.
